What is the 50/30/20 rule?

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple way to split your income after tax. About half goes to needs (housing, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt payments), 30% to wants (eating out, hobbies, subscriptions, travel) and 20% to savings and paying off debt faster.

It works because it's easy to remember and doesn't require tracking every coffee. If your rent alone is close to half your income, a 60/20/20 or 70/20/10 split is more realistic. The goal is to save something every month, not to hit a perfect number.

How to use your result

Compare the needs figure with what you actually spend on fixed costs. If you're above it, look at the biggest items first (housing, car, phone plan) rather than small daily spending. Then set the savings amount up as an automatic transfer on payday so it happens before you can spend it.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 50/30/20 rule based on gross or net income?

Net income: the money that actually lands in your account after tax. If you have pre-tax savings like a pension, you can count those toward the 20%.

What if my needs are more than 50%?

That's common where rent is high. Try a 60/20/20 or 70/20/10 split, and focus on lowering your biggest fixed cost over time.

Does paying off debt count as savings?

Minimum payments are a need. Anything you pay above the minimum counts toward the 20% savings bucket.
