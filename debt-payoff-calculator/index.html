How the debt payoff calculator works

Each month, interest is added to your balance (your yearly rate divided by 12) and your payment is subtracted. The calculator repeats this until the balance reaches zero, so you see the real number of months and the total interest you'll pay.

The extra-payment field shows how much faster you get out of debt by paying a little more. Because extra money goes straight to the balance, it cuts the interest you pay in every following month.

Snowball or avalanche?

With several debts, the avalanche method pays extra on the highest interest rate first and saves the most money. The snowball method pays off the smallest balance first, which gives quick wins and keeps many people motivated. Both work; the best one is the one you stick with.

Frequently asked questions

Does this work for credit cards and loans?

Yes. It works for any debt with a fixed yearly interest rate and a fixed monthly payment, like credit cards, car loans and personal loans.

Why does it say my payment doesn't cover the interest?

If your monthly payment is lower than the interest added each month, the balance never goes down. You need to pay more than the interest to make progress.

Is the result exact?

It's a close estimate. Your lender may calculate interest daily or add fees, so the real numbers can differ slightly.
