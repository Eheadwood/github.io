How to quote a freelance project

Break the project into tasks and estimate the hours for each one. Small, concrete tasks are easier to estimate than one big guess, and they show the client what they're paying for. Multiply the hours by your hourly rate to get the base fee.

Then add a buffer. Projects almost always take longer than planned: extra emails, a slow round of feedback, a file that needs redoing. A buffer of 10–25% protects your hourly rate without padding any single task. Add expenses you pay on the client's behalf, and tax or VAT if you charge it.

Fixed price or hourly?

A fixed project price is easier for clients to approve, and if you work efficiently you keep the upside. The risk is scope creep. Protect yourself by listing what's included, how many revision rounds there are, and your hourly rate for anything extra.

Why ask for a deposit?

A deposit, often 30–50%, confirms the client is committed, covers your early work and reduces the risk of not getting paid at all. Many freelancers start work only once the deposit has arrived, and invoice the balance on delivery.

Frequently asked questions

How much buffer should I add to a project quote?

Many freelancers add 10–25% depending on how well-defined the project is. Use more for new clients or vague briefs, less for repeat work you know well.

Should I show the client my hours?

You don't have to. Many freelancers show tasks and a total price instead of hours, which keeps the focus on the result rather than the time.

How big should a deposit be?

30–50% up front is common for project work. For long projects, split payment into milestones instead of one deposit and one final invoice.
