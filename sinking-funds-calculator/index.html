What is a sinking fund?

A sinking fund is money you set aside a little at a time for an expense you know is coming: car insurance, holidays, Christmas, a new phone, the vet. Instead of one big bill wrecking your budget in a single month, you spread the cost over the months before it's due.

The idea is simple: divide what you still need by the number of months left. If car insurance of 900 is due in 8 months and you've saved 150, you need 750 more, or 93.75 a month.

How to use several sinking funds

Most people have more than one. Add each expected cost above with when you'll need it, and the calculator gives you one total to transfer each payday. Many people keep it all in one savings account and track the split in a spreadsheet, rather than opening an account for every goal.

Start with the expenses that surprise you most often: annual bills, car costs, gifts and travel. Once those are covered, irregular spending stops feeling like an emergency.

Sinking fund or emergency fund?

An emergency fund is for things you can't predict, like losing your job. A sinking fund is for costs you can see coming, even if the exact amount varies. You want both, but keep them separate so you don't spend your safety net on a holiday.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I put in my sinking funds?

Take what you still need for each goal and divide by the months until you need it. Add the results together for your monthly total.

Should sinking funds be in separate accounts?

Not necessarily. Many people use one savings account and track each fund in a spreadsheet. Separate accounts can help if you're tempted to dip into the money.

What are common sinking fund categories?

Car insurance and repairs, holidays, Christmas and birthdays, annual subscriptions, home maintenance, medical costs and pet care.
