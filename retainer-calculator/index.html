How to price a monthly retainer

A retainer is a fixed monthly fee for an agreed amount of your time or a set of ongoing tasks. The simplest way to price it is to start from your normal hourly rate, multiply by the hours you include, and give a small discount, often 5–15%, in return for the client committing for several months. That steady income is worth something to you, and the discount makes it easy to say yes.

Don't forget the hidden time. Calls, emails, reporting and planning add up every month, and if they aren't priced in, your real hourly rate quietly drops. Add them as admin hours so the fee covers them.

What if the client uses fewer or more hours?

If the client uses fewer hours than included, your real hourly rate goes up: you're paid for availability. If they use more, set a clear overage rate (usually your full hourly rate) and agree whether unused hours roll over to next month. Most freelancers allow no rollover or a small, capped rollover to stop hours piling up.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I discount a retainer?

Many freelancers give 5–15% off their normal hourly rate in return for a commitment of three months or more. Bigger discounts only make sense for longer commitments or much larger blocks of hours.

Should unused retainer hours roll over?

It's up to you, but most freelancers either don't allow rollover or cap it, for example at 25% of the monthly hours for one month. Put the rule in your contract.

How do I charge for extra hours on a retainer?

Agree an overage rate in advance, usually your full hourly rate without the retainer discount, and invoice extra hours at the end of the month.
