Why your rate needs to be higher than you think

A salaried employee is paid for every hour they work, plus holidays. As a freelancer you only earn on billable hours, and you pay your own costs: software, equipment, insurance, accounting and time off. That's why simply dividing a yearly salary by 2,080 hours gives a rate that's far too low.

This calculator starts from the income you want, adds your business costs, and divides by the hours you can realistically bill after time off and non-billable work. The result is your minimum rate: the point where you hit your goal, with no margin for slow months.

Checking your rate against the market

If your minimum rate is well above what clients in your field pay, look at the inputs you control: raise the share of billable hours with better processes, cut costs, or move toward higher-value clients and projects. If it's below the market rate, you probably have room to charge more.

Frequently asked questions

How many billable hours does a freelancer have?

Many freelancers can bill 50–70% of their working hours. The rest goes to admin, finding clients, invoicing and learning.

Should I include tax in my income goal?

Enter the income you want before tax. Then set money aside for tax from every payment you receive.

Is this the rate I should charge?

It's your minimum. Many freelancers add a buffer of 10–30% for slow months and unexpected costs, and check it against market rates in their field.
