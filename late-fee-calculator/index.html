How invoice late fees are calculated

There are three common ways to charge for late payment. A percentage per month (often 1–1.5%) is simple to explain: a 1.5% monthly fee on a 1,500 invoice is 22.50 for each month it's overdue. A yearly interest rate is charged per day: divide the rate by 365, multiply by the amount and by the days overdue. A flat fee is a fixed amount once the invoice is late, sometimes combined with interest.

The calculator counts only the days after any grace period you offer. Monthly fees are charged pro rata here (45 days = 1.5 months); if your terms say each started month counts in full, round up.

What the rules allow

You can usually only charge a late fee if it was agreed in advance, for example in your contract or the payment terms on the invoice. In the EU, the Late Payment Directive lets businesses charge statutory interest on late business-to-business payments at the central bank reference rate plus at least 8 percentage points, plus a fixed minimum compensation of 40 euros for recovery costs. In the US, rules and maximum rates vary by state. Check what applies where you and your client are based.

Getting paid faster

Late fees work best as a deterrent. State your terms clearly on every invoice, send a friendly reminder a few days before the due date, and follow up on the day after. Many freelancers find that a short, polite reminder gets most invoices paid without ever applying the fee.

Frequently asked questions

What is a normal late fee on an invoice?

Many businesses charge 1–1.5% per month or a small flat fee. Whatever you choose, put it in your contract or payment terms before you invoice.

Can I charge a late fee if it wasn't agreed?

Usually not, except where the law gives you a statutory right, like interest on late business-to-business payments in the EU. Check the rules where you and your client are based.

How do I calculate interest on an overdue invoice?

Multiply the invoice amount by the yearly rate, divide by 365, and multiply by the number of days overdue after any grace period.
